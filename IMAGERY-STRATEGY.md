## SECTION 2: IMAGERY STYLE GUIDE

### PHOTOGRAPHY STYLE FRAMEWORK

**Primary Style: Contemporary Luxury Architecture**
- Approach: Clean lines, generous white space, sophisticated composition
- Emphasis: Light, transparency, clarity, precision
- Aesthetic: Modern minimalist with warm human touches
- Comparable Brands: B&O, Leica, Patek Philippe (visual precision and premium positioning)

**Secondary Styles:**
- **Technical/Detail:** Studio-quality macro photography showing engineering precision
- **Lifestyle:** Aspirational documentary style (not overly posed; natural moments)
- **Documentary:** Manufacturing/process photography showing craft and expertise

### COLOR GRADING PHILOSOPHY

**Overall Approach:** Timeless, sophisticated, slightly warm with cool accents

**Core Color Palette:**
- **Whites & Neutrals:** Crisp whites (not yellow-shifted), warm grays, cool grays
- **Accent Colors:** Deep charcoal, navy, sophisticated black
- **Warm Tones:** Golden hour light (3500-4500K), amber accents
- **Cool Tones:** Professional daylight (5000-5500K), cool blue accents (selective)
- **Secondary Colors:** Deep green (natural elements), warm wood tones

**Color Grading Rules:**
1. Whites should feel pure and clean (avoid yellow undertone)
2. Shadows should retain detail (avoid pure black; use dark gray)
3. Midtones should feel warm and inviting (2500-3000K for interiors)
4. Highlights should show glass clarity (bright but not blown)
5. Skin tones warm and natural (not overly saturated)
6. Greens rich but natural (if used; avoid neon or oversaturated)

**Saturation Approach:**
- Slightly desaturated overall (10-15% below natural) for sophisticated feel
- Selective saturation in accent colors and brand elements
- Avoid highly saturated or "punchy" colors (conflicts with luxury positioning)

### LIGHTING STRATEGY

**Golden Hour Priority (Best Practice)**
- Schedule outdoor shoots for golden hour (1 hour before sunset / 1 hour after sunrise)
- Benefits: Warm color temperature, directional light creates dimension, flattering on subjects
- Creates sense of premium time investment and expertise

**Indoor Artificial Lighting**
- **Warm Accent Lights:** 2700K LED for welcoming, premium feel
- **Task/Neutral Lighting:** 4000K for professional spaces
- **Backlight:** High-quality backlighting through glass to show transparency
- **Avoid:** Harsh overhead fluorescents, visible hot spots, uneven lighting

**Studio/Controlled Lighting (Technical Images)**
- Three-point lighting fundamental (key, fill, backlight)
- Key light: 45° angle, creating dimension
- Fill light: Subtle, reducing shadows without eliminating them
- Backlight: Emphasizing glass clarity and surface quality
- Aim: Professional, controlled, showing mastery

**Natural Light Optimization**
- Large windows as primary light source when possible
- Position subjects perpendicular to window light for dimension
- Supplement with warm accent lighting to prevent cool shadows
- Show daylight quality through glass (demonstrates transparency)

### COMPOSITION PRINCIPLES

**Fundamental Framing Rules**

1. **Rule of Thirds (Modified)**
   - Position key subjects at intersections or along thirds lines
   - Allow space for environmental context (don't center everything)
   - Asymmetrical composition creates sophistication

2. **Leading Lines**
   - Use architectural lines, glass edges, sight lines to guide viewer
   - Diagonal composition more dynamic than grid-aligned
   - Window lines, horizon lines, vertical glass features

3. **Depth & Layering**
   - Include foreground (sharp), middle ground (subject), background (softer)
   - Glass features should show depth and transparency
   - Avoid flat, single-plane compositions

4. **Negative Space**
   - Embrace white space (luxury aesthetic principle)
   - Don't overcrowd frame; let elements breathe
   - Space suggests sophistication and control

5. **Framing Within Frame**
   - Use architectural elements to frame subject (glass frame within image)
   - Windows, doorways, glass partitions create natural frames
   - Emphasizes glass quality and integration

6. **Focal Point Clarity**
   - One clear primary focal point (viewer's eye knows where to look)
   - Supporting elements enhance but don't distract
   - Avoid visual ambiguity (luxury demands clarity)

### CONSISTENCY ACROSS SITE

**Visual Coherence Checklist:**
- [ ] All hero images: 16:9 aspect ratio (except specified detail images)
- [ ] All images: Color graded consistently (warm/cool temperature aligned)
- [ ] All images: Professional photography quality (sharp focus, proper exposure)
- [ ] All people photos: Confident, professional expressions (avoid overly posed)
- [ ] All glass features: Pristine clarity (no dust, fingerprints, distortions)
- [ ] All architectural: Clean lines and intentional composition
- [ ] All detail/macro: Studio quality with consistent lighting direction

**File Management Standards:**
- Format: RGB JPEG or PNG (for web; not CMYK)
- Resolution: Minimum 1920px width for hero images (scalable)
- File size: Optimized for web (typically 150-300KB after compression)
- Naming convention: `glas-expert-[type]-[number]-[descriptor].jpg`
  - Example: `glas-expert-hero-01-penthouse-office.jpg`
  - Example: `glas-expert-detail-03-edge-precision.jpg`

**Responsive Design Considerations:**
- Hero images: Provide versions cropped for mobile (vertical emphasis)
- Detail images: Maintain 1:1 or square aspect for consistency
- All images: Maintain quality across retina (@2x) displays

## SECTION 5: PREMIUM AESTHETIC CONSISTENCY GUIDELINES

### VISUAL HIERARCHY & BRAND IMPRESSION

**What Premium Glass Imagery Should Communicate:**

1. **Clarity & Precision**
   - Glass must be immaculate (zero visible defects, dust, fingerprints)
   - Edges must appear perfectly finished
   - Reflections should be intentional and designed
   - Every detail should feel controlled and deliberate

2. **Luxury Through Understatement**
   - Avoid over-styling or excessive decoration
   - Premium brands show restraint (Scandinavian/Japanese design principles)
   - Negative space is desirable (signals sophistication and control)
   - Minimalism often equals luxury

3. **Quality Material Integration**
   - Glass should integrate seamlessly with premium materials
   - Surrounding elements: natural stone, high-end metals, quality wood
   - Avoid cheap or plastic-looking materials in frame
   - Show how glass elevates entire space

4. **Human Presence (When Included)**
   - People should appear confident and comfortable (not awkward)
   - Professional attire: high-quality fabrics, excellent fit
   - Expressions: engaged, intelligent, satisfied (never stressed or confused)
   - Diversity encouraged; ensure inclusive representation
   - Avoid token/artificial inclusion (must feel authentic)

5. **Environmental Context**
   - Show how spaces function (people working, living, enjoying)
   - Environmental light quality matters (morning light different from afternoon)
   - Context establishes scale and significance
   - Avoid sterile or vacant spaces (except for detail shots)

6. **Technical Credibility**
   - Manufacturing imagery must show modern, clean facilities
   - Equipment should appear advanced and well-maintained
   - Quality control processes visible and rigorous
   - Precision evident in every detail

### COLOR PSYCHOLOGY FOR LUXURY GLASS MARKET

**Color Strategy (by market segment):**

**Residential/Luxury Homes:**
- Warm, inviting tones (golden hour preferred)
- Cream, warm gray, soft accent colors
- Natural materials (wood, stone) complement
- Creates aspirational "dream home" feeling

**Commercial/Office:**
- Cool, professional tones (neutral daylight 5000K)
- Clean whites, professional grays
- Green accents (biophilic, calming)
- Inspires productivity and confidence

**Hospitality/Spa:**
- Warm, soothing tones (2700K lighting preferred)
- Soft grays, warm whites, subtle pastels
- Natural materials dominant
- Promotes relaxation and indulgence

**Technical/Manufacturing:**
- Clean, clinical tones (neutral 5000K whites)
- Metallic accents (aluminum, stainless steel)
- Minimal color distraction
- Emphasizes precision and control

### LIGHTING MOOD BY APPLICATION

| Space Type | Lighting Approach | Color Temperature | Mood Created |
|---|---|---|---|
| Luxury Residential | Golden hour + warm accents | 2700K + golden light | Inviting, aspirational, intimate |
| Modern Office | Natural daylight + neutral accents | 4500-5000K | Professional, energetic, clear |
| Spa/Hospitality | Warm indirect lighting | 2700K | Luxurious, relaxing, indulgent |
| Architectural Detail | Professional studio lighting | 5000K-5500K | Precise, engineered, trustworthy |
| Manufacturing | Clean facility lighting | 5000K | Clinical, controlled, professional |

### COMPOSITION CONSISTENCY RULES

**All Hero Images Must:**
- [ ] Have clear focal point (viewer's eye knows where to look)
- [ ] Show glass feature prominently but not overwhelmingly
- [ ] Include environmental context (show scale/significance)
- [ ] Demonstrate light quality (how light interacts with glass)
- [ ] Have clean composition (rule of thirds applied)
- [ ] Show human comfort or engagement (if people present)

**All Detail Images Must:**
- [ ] Display pristine surface quality
- [ ] Show precision and engineering excellence
- [ ] Use controlled studio lighting
- [ ] Eliminate all distractions (clean white or neutral background)
- [ ] Magnify importance through macro/close perspective
- [ ] Be symmetrical or strongly compositionally balanced

**All Lifestyle Images Must:**
- [ ] Show people engaged and comfortable
- [ ] Display real-world functionality (not sterile/empty)
- [ ] Include premium surrounding materials/furnishings
- [ ] Demonstrate light quality and how glass enhances space
- [ ] Convey aspirational lifestyle (achievable luxury)
- [ ] Show intentional design (nothing accidental)

### AVOIDING COMMON PREMIUM BRAND MISTAKES

**DON'T:**
- [ ] Use overly saturated colors (conflicts with luxury)
- [ ] Include visible branding/watermarks (integrate subtly or avoid)
- [ ] Show people in uncomfortable poses or expressions
- [ ] Use harsh overhead lighting (appears cheap and institutional)
- [ ] Include plastic or low-quality materials in frame
- [ ] Center everything (asymmetry often more sophisticated)
- [ ] Overuse special effects or filters (suggests lack of confidence)
- [ ] Mix multiple lighting color temperatures (inconsistent/amateur)
- [ ] Show dirty, dusty, or imperfect glass (undermines positioning)
- [ ] Include too many elements/text in frame (confusion = not premium)

**DO:**
- [ ] Embrace negative space and clean compositions
- [ ] Use natural light whenever possible
- [ ] Show precise craftsmanship and attention to detail
- [ ] Include high-quality, recognizable materials/brands
- [ ] Use warm, inviting color tones (especially in residential)
- [ ] Emphasize clarity, light, and transparency
- [ ] Show real people in genuine comfort/confidence
- [ ] Maintain consistent color grading across all images
- [ ] Allow glass to be the star (not overwhelmed by other elements)
- [ ] Invest in professional-quality production (quality visible)
