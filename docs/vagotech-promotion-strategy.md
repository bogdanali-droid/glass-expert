## Self-critique applied

- **Failure mode: Public procurement reality** — CFR Calatori and STB are public entities; large contracts go through SEAP/SICAP formal tenders, not a website form. Digital marketing cannot "close" a €1.6M public deal. **Mitigation:** digital is reframed as *demand-generation + credibility + spec-in + qualification*; the winnable near-term revenue is **private OEMs** (Electroputere, Astra Vagoane) and **private export buyers** (DE/RS/BG/HU/MD tier suppliers). Public operators are a spec-in long-game.
- **Failure mode: Incumbent suppliers + re-homologation cost** — operators already have homologated glazing suppliers; switching means re-certification. **Mitigation:** message is *certified second source + supply-risk reduction (dual sourcing) + single-supplier accountability + predictable lead time* — not a frontal price attack.
- **Failure mode: Unrealistic Google Ads ROI** — search volume for rail-glass terms (e.g. "geam vagon EN 15152") is near-zero. **Mitigation:** Google Ads downgraded from primary to a *secondary* channel (brand defense + retargeting + capturing the little intent that exists); budget reallocated to LinkedIn ABM + direct outbound.
- **Failure mode: Brand/technical credibility** — the older internal lead doc cited *wrong* standards (EN 12288/14752) and a false "US delivery 4–6 weeks" angle. Railway engineers verify claims. **Mitigation:** correct standards everywhere (EN 15152 / EN 15153 / EN 12600), and **EN 45545 — fire & smoke — flagged as a must-validate prerequisite** (it gates most EU rail tenders). Delivery advantage = **European proximity (Timisoara hub)**, not US shipping.
- **Failure mode: Channel fragmentation on a new-division budget** — six channels at once = none funded properly. **Mitigation:** 90-day plan is phased (Foundation -> Activation -> Scale), not simultaneous.

**Assumptions to validate with the client (before spend):**
1. **EN 45545 capability** — does the plant hold or can it obtain fire/smoke certification? *This is a go/no-go for most EU rail tenders.* (How: confirm with Glas Expert technical/lab.)
2. **ICP priority** — agree that private OEMs + export are the near-term revenue focus; public operators are spec-in/long-game. (How: 30-min alignment call.)
3. **Willingness-to-pay** — premium-EU positioning assumes buyers pay over TR/PL/CN low-cost suppliers when quality/delivery risk is perceived. (How: validate in first 5 discovery calls.)
4. **Marketing budget band** — three options provided; my recommendation for a new division is the Standard tier.

**Changelog vs v1:** Google Ads demoted to secondary (near-zero search intent); added public-tender vs private-OEM framing; corrected standards + added EN 45545; removed false "US delivery" angle (EU proximity instead); 90-day plan re-phased; value proposition recentred on in-house/single-supplier/predictability; market figures marked as *addressable*, not forecast.

We position as a **credible new entrant with a proven process pedigree**, de-risking the buyer with **free samples and integration test panels**. This is more persuasive to a rail CTO than overclaiming.

### 1.2 Differentiating key message

**Primary message (one line):**
> "European rail glazing, engineered in-house and delivered on time — a certified second source that takes supply risk off your fleet program."

**Message pillars (use across all channels):**
1. **Certified, not commodity** — EN 15152 (windscreens), EN 15153 (windows & doors), EN 12600 (impact); documented compliance per order. *(EN 45545 fire/smoke to be confirmed and added once validated.)*
2. **Single-supplier accountability** — in-house tempering, lamination, curved shaping, custom edge work; no sub-contracting, no spec drift.
3. **Predictable delivery from Europe** — Timisoara processing hub, CEE proximity, 6–8 week standard lead time; one accountable partner from drawing to delivery.
4. **De-risk your fleet** — second-source / dual-sourcing reduces single-supplier exposure; free sample + integration test panel before any commitment.

**What we do NOT lead with:** lowest price (we lose to TR/PL/CN), or raw delivery speed (in rail, certification > speed). Price and speed are *secondary* supports, not the headline.

### 1.3 Channel mix & budget allocation (recommended)

Allocation reflects the reality that **search intent is tiny** and **the buyer is reached via people, not keywords**. ABM + outbound + LinkedIn carry the load; ads support.

| Channel | % of budget | Role in the funnel |
|---------|:-----------:|--------------------|
| **LinkedIn (ABM, InMail, company page, thought-leadership)** | **35%** | Reach named decision-makers; build authority; primary demand-gen |
| **Direct outbound (email sequences + sales follow-up)** | **30%** | 1:1 to OEM/operator procurement & technical leads; spec-in motion |
| **SEO & technical content (pillar pages, spec sheets, case material)** | **20%** | Long-term inbound, credibility assets, sales enablement |
| **Google Ads (Search brand + niche intent, Display retargeting)** | **10%** | Brand defense, capture the little intent, retarget site visitors |
| **Trade/industry presence (associations, directories, 1 event)** | **5%** | Listings, credibility, warm intros (UNIFE/AFER/trade press) |

> Rationale: on this niche, paid search cannot be the engine — there isn't enough volume. We invest where the rail buyer actually is: LinkedIn, inbox, and technical content they reference during evaluation.
